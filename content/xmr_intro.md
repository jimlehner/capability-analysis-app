## XmR charts: The most useful type of process behavior chart

When a dataset is the by-product of a process or system and it retains its time-ordered sequence, the most effective way to make sense
of it is the XmR chart, the most useful type of process behavior chart. By characterizing process behavior as predictable or 
unpredictable using calculated limits (process limits), process behavior charts allow us to determine when it is economical to 
take actions to improve a process and when it is economical to leave a process alone.

[FIGURE_1]

XmR charts are composed of two subplots, the chart of individual values—the X chart—and the chart of the associated moving ranges—the 
mR chart. The defining features of an XmR chart are the Upper Process Limit (UPL), the Lower Process Limit (LPL), and the Upper Range 
Limit (URL). The UPL and LPL define how large or small a value must be before it represents a departure from the historic mean on the
X chart. The URL defines how large a moving range must be before it represents a departure from the average moving range on the mR chart.

[FIGURE_2]

When one or more values on an XmR chart is greater than the UPL, less than the LPL, greater than the URL, or some combination of all 
three conditions, this is evidence that both common causes of routine variation and assignable causes of exceptional variation influence 
process behavior. In these instances, the process is characterized as unpredictable. To improve an unpredictable process the assignable 
causes of exceptional variation must be eliminated. Although few in number, they are dominant in their effect. They cause a process to 
bounce and shift in unpredictable ways. This unpredictable behavior will continue until the assignable causes are eliminated.

When all of the values on an XmR chart fall within the process limits, this is evidence that only common causes of routine variation 
influence process behavior. In these instances, the process is characterized as predictable. Although common causes are many in number, 
they are minimal in their effect. If deemed economically viable, the improvement of a predictable process requires reengineering through 
the introduction of new technology, new equipment, new materials, and new procedures. Although this is contrary to conventional wisdom, 
it has been proven in practice time and time again.

[FIGURE_3]

Process behavior charts generally, and the XmR chart specifically, are an operational definition for how to operate a process at its full 
potential (high yield and low cost). They ensures that, rather than waste time performing an FMEA, improving the measurement process, or 
brainstorming what problem to work on next, the process tells you what to fix and when to fix it. No other tool is more capable or competent
in this task.
